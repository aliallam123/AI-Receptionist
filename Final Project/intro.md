Introduction and Background
Customers expect instant, 24/7 service, yet most SMEs operate with tight staffing and budget constraints. Traditional receptionists cover office hours only and scale poorly. The result is a coverage gap that suppresses revenue. BT Business quantifies the damage, with £1,200 lost per missed call and £90 million annually for UK SMEs (BT Business, 2024). Compounding the problem, 85 percent of unanswered callers never retry, so a single missed interaction often becomes a lost customer (NewVoiceMedia, 2018). Addressing this requires more than extended hours. It demands a different approach.
Voice AI is often presented as the remedy, yet many tools remain passive, documenting interactions rather than completing tasks. Følstad and Brandtzæg (2020) define conversational agency as the capacity to pursue user goals and complete tasks in real time, provided users understand scope and limits. Luger and Sellen (2016) show that agents acting beyond perceived competence or concealing constraints trigger distrust and abandonment. This body of work suggests that successful AI service agents must balance proactive capability with explicit governance - a design challenge underexplored in existing voice-AI implementations for resource-constrained organisations. For SMEs, this tension converts a technical gap into a strategic opportunity to design agents that act transparently and deliver outcomes.
This project investigates an AI Receptionist for UK SMEs that answers inbound calls, recognises intent, confirms details, and triggers actions such as bookings or notifications. The system integrates real-time speech recognition, intent classification, and workflow automation through Twilio Voice, Whisper, and n8n. It prioritises low latency, strong security, and multi-tenant scalability on AWS. The design uses event-driven services and clear handoff rules so complex or sensitive queries can transfer seamlessly to staff with full context retained.
Project aim. Design, develop, and evaluate an AI Receptionist that delivers enterprise-grade engagement at SME-appropriate cost. Evaluation focuses on three objectives. First, technical feasibility, achieving sub two-second end-to-end latency on AWS by using Lambda for elastic compute and CloudFront to minimise network delays. Second, business impact, quantifying reductions in call handling cost and improvements in customer satisfaction relative to baseline. Third, trust and scalability, assessing caller acceptance, transparency mechanisms such as confirmations for higher-risk actions, and the viability of secure multi-tenant deployment. The initial sector focus includes dental practices, law firms, and real estate agencies, where timely booking and triage directly affect revenue.
The significance is strategic. SMEs compete on responsiveness, yet cannot staff around the clock. A well-governed voice agent can extend service without eroding trust by acting transparently, escalating when needed, and protecting data. Evidence from practice is encouraging. One UK dental practice reported a 40 percent reduction in administrative workload and a 30 percent improvement in patient satisfaction after introducing a 24/7 AI receptionist (Callin.io, 2025). This project tests whether such outcomes generalise under rigorous evaluation, and shows how accessible AI can level competition for smaller firms.
